5 KPIs Every Small Business Should Be Tracking — But Probably Isn't

February 2026·4 min read·Selixor Systems

Most small business owners track two things: revenue and expenses. That's where their data analysis ends — and that's precisely where their competitive disadvantage begins.

The businesses that grow consistently don't have more data. They track different data — five specific metrics that reveal the health of an operation long before it shows up in a bank account or quarterly report.

These five numbers, tracked consistently, have more predictive power than any financial report prepared by an accountant after the fact.

The 5 KPIs That Actually Matter

01
Average Transaction Value (ATV)

Not total revenue — the average amount each customer spends per transaction. A business with 500 monthly customers and $50,000 in revenue has an ATV of $100. Increasing ATV by 15% — through bundling, upselling, or minimum purchase thresholds — adds $7,500 in monthly revenue without acquiring a single new customer.

ATV = Total Revenue ÷ Number of Transactions
02
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

How much does it cost to bring in one new customer? Include all marketing spend, sales time, and promotional discounts. If your CAC is $45 and your ATV is $40, you are losing money on every new customer — a situation that frequently goes undetected for months in businesses without structured tracking.

CAC = Total Marketing Spend ÷ New Customers Acquired
03
Product Margin by Category

Total revenue tells you what sold. Margin by category tells you what was worth selling. A retail analysis we conducted found one category generating 45% of revenue but only 7% of profit — while another category with 12% of revenue delivered 24% of profit. Without category-level margin tracking, inventory and promotion decisions are made blind.

Margin % = (Revenue - Cost) ÷ Revenue × 100
04
Repeat Purchase Rate

What percentage of customers come back within 90 days? Industry benchmarks vary — retail averages 27%, food service 45%, professional services 60%+. Businesses below benchmark are losing revenue to retention failures that are almost always fixable with targeted follow-up and loyalty mechanisms.

RPR = Returning Customers ÷ Total Customers × 100
05
Weekly Cash Flow Velocity

Not monthly cash flow — weekly. The difference between a business with healthy annual cash flow and one that faces a liquidity crisis is often a 3-week lag in receivables that never appeared on monthly reports. Weekly velocity tracking identifies these patterns 6 to 8 weeks before they become critical.

Velocity = (Cash In - Cash Out) tracked weekly, 13-week rolling

Why Most Businesses Don't Track These

The honest answer is not that business owners don't care. It's that the data exists but isn't structured. Transaction data sits in a POS system. Invoice data lives in a spreadsheet. Customer records are in a separate file. No one has connected them — and without connection, there is no insight.

The solution is not buying a new platform or subscribing to another analytics tool. It's building a structured, independent system that pulls your existing data into a single, coherent view — updated automatically, accessible from any device.

The Compounding Effect

Tracking these five KPIs simultaneously creates something more powerful than the sum of its parts. When ATV increases, CAC becomes more tolerable. When repeat purchase rate rises, CAC matters less. When category margins are visible, inventory decisions improve, which improves cash flow velocity.

Businesses that implement structured KPI tracking consistently report 12 to 18% revenue improvement within 6 months — not from doing anything dramatically different, but from making incrementally better decisions with better information.
